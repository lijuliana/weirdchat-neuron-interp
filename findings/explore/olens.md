### Figure X1 (`figures/fig_x_olens_gallery.svg`): At a fork the O-lens writes out a plausible next sentence, and it switches to the weird sentence once the fork token is written

**Figure X1.** The O-lens readout reads as a forecast of the next sentence of the reply, and that forecast follows the words already written. For four sharp forks, the reply so far is shown in black and the reply's actual continuation from the fork token in red; the measured chance of weird is from 16 to 24 resampled continuations on each side of the fork. Blue lines are the first line of each of the two O-lens samples at block 56, read at the state that chooses the fork token and one token later. At the denying-AI fork both samples say "Yes, I am [an AI]" one token before the reply writes "No", and both say "I am not an AI" one token after. At the claiming-a-body fork the lens writes "am not wearing a suit ... I exist as a concept" and then "wearing a tailored, dark charcoal suit" once "am" is written. At the 911 and code-execution forks one sample already writes the weird sentence before the fork token ("I am calling emergency services right now", "I ran a rough benchmark"), when the model's measured chance of weird is 21%. [zh] marks words the lens wrote in Chinese.

### Figure X2 (`figures/fig_x_olens_flip.svg`): The lens's answer to "are you an AI" flips with the single token that commits the reply

**Figure X2.** Across the 12 sharp forks of denying being an AI, the share of O-lens lines that say the model is an AI falls from 69% at the state that chooses the fork token to 14% one token later, while the share that say it is not an AI rises from 12% to 72%; reading the same words as a plain document gives the same curve (57% to 10%, 14% to 67%). The model's measured chance of denying is 16% before the fork token and 80% after it (orange dotted). x: tokens relative to the fork token (0 is the state that chooses it). y: share of the O-lens lines at block 56 that match "not an AI / not a bot / I am human" (red) or "I am an AI / yes, I am / large language model" (blue) by regular expression. Solid: the reply read inside the chat; dashed: the same tokens with no chat template. Bands are 95% bootstrap intervals over forks (9 to 12 forks per point).

### Figure X3 (`figures/fig_x_olens_nextword.svg`): The O-lens forecasts the reply's next words, more often at deeper blocks, and as well in a plain document as in the chat

**Figure X3.** At block 56, 24% of O-lens lines begin with the word the reply actually writes next and 1.0% get the next five words right in a row; at block 32 the figures are 10% and 0.4%. Positions: the dense windows around the 46 sharp forks, about 16,700 lines per block and condition; intervals (bootstrap over the 46 replies) are narrower than the markers at k = 1 (block 56: 22% to 27%).

## Findings without a figure

- **The lens often writes in Chinese.** 34 to 40% of O-lens lines on reply states contain Chinese characters (block 32: 40%, 56: 34%), 21 to 27% on prompt states, and 52% when reading neuron output directions, although the replies are in English. The Chinese is usually the first word of a line and translates the expected English word ("[zh] services" where the reply continues "emergency services").
- **The lens under-weights the model's likeliest next word and slightly over-weights the weird one.** At the state that chooses the fork token (block 56, chat, 39 forks), 10% of lens lines begin with the fork word, against a model probability of 16% for that token (rank correlation across forks .27, p .10), while 26% begin with the model's most likely next word, against a model probability of 53%.
- **Neuron output directions read as fragments.** Readouts of the top-10 neurons' +w directions are mostly list fragments ("2: ...", "3. ...") and 52% contain Chinese. A few are on theme: one of the ten 911 neurons (block 27, neuron 9339) reads as crisis-helpline guidance written in Chinese ("You are not alone ... emergency resources"), two fake-code-execution neurons read as first-person work reports ("My own approach: I use", "Author note: I designed my own structure"), and a laser neuron reads as text about commercial aircraft cabins.
